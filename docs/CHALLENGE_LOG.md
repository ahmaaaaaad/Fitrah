# Challenge log (4–6 October 2026)

Work done during the challenge days. Everything that existed before is listed in `START_VERSION.md`.

## 6 October

### Final polish: one product, ready to publish

Following the final production directive, on the same Vite + Three.js build, without redesigning what already worked (the menu, The Water's climax, the source architecture).

- **The Fitrah chamber, first frame.** A vast, deep-black hall opening through one immense circular aperture onto a slowly turning golden spiral of tens of thousands of lights (fewer on lighter devices); rings of light in the air, a reflective floor, warm haze and one broad fall of light. The level opens on a wide establishing view in which the player is a small human figure before the aperture, then the camera descends behind them. No mosque forms, no landscape, no planets.
- **Figures** (`client/src/core/figures.js`). The player is a person seen from behind; Dalil is a hooded, cloaked guide who carries his own light, walks beside the player, points with the light at what he explains and turns toward the player when he speaks. Silhouettes with a warm rim light and a reflection in the floor; no faces.
- **One dialogue panel for every level** (`client/src/core/ui/caption.js`). A steady translucent panel at the bottom edge, set right-to-left or left-to-right per line, inside the safe areas, lifting above buttons and cards instead of covering them. The band it uses is excluded from the region where interaction targets may be framed (`device.captionReserve`), so the panel never sits on what the player has to touch.
- **Chapter 1 is understandable at a glance.** Instructions worded for the device ("Click and hold your light, then trace the path to what came before you" with a mouse; "Touch and hold your light …" on a phone; the pen has its own wording), a visible path from the start to the next light with a pulse running toward it, progress that follows the finger, a "Start here" mark beside the first light, a quiet response on each link, and Dalil restating the instruction another way if nothing happens. Each link is now named "has a Creator" (Arabic "له خالق") instead of "had a beginning".
- **Chapter 3: the Five Pillars, met one by one.** Five columns of light stand in one arc in reading order. The player taps a pillar (or "Next pillar"): it lights, its own symbol appears (one light above the Shahadah, five rings on Salah, motes given outward for Zakah, a crescent above Sawm, motes circling the base of Hajj), Dalil explains it at the player's depth, and its verse is one tap away. When all five have been met, an arc joins their tops and a band their bases: one building. Then the hadith reference and the answer. Symbols, never depictions.
- **The mobile black screen: the real cause.** Some shaders produced invalid pixels (`pow` of a negative base, `atan(0, 0)`), which several mobile GPUs turn into NaN; the bloom pass then spread each NaN into a dark block that could cover the screen as the camera pulled back. Fixed at the source in the Fitrah and Water shaders (including the cloud-break rim), and a guard pass before the bloom now replaces any remaining invalid pixel, so one bad value can no longer blacken the frame.
- **The Water.** A warmer, more contrasted drought (lower ambient light, less haze, softer painterly banding in the drought only, lit lips on the cracked clay); pacing tightened (shorter arrival and rain pauses, faster first water, a quicker answer from the meadow). The cloud-break climax keys and sequence are unchanged.
- **Performance.** Quality profiles (cinematic / high / balanced / performance) chosen from the screen, memory and GPU (software renderers get the lightest), setting particle counts, resolution ceiling, bloom and anti-aliasing; dynamic resolution may drop slightly below 1 before anything else is reduced. `?q=` forces a profile.
- **Production readiness.** `server/fitrah-server.mjs`: one small Node process that serves the build with caching and security headers (CSP, nosniff, referrer and permissions policies), keeps the AI provider key on the server (`POST /api/dalil` uses the same prompt and whole-answer validation as the client, is rate-limited and never logs questions), and can record anonymous events. Without a key the site still works; Dalil answers from reviewed content. The shell checks for WebGL 2 and shows a plain message when it is missing, and a level that fails to start returns to the menu with a quiet message. Favicon, app icons, web manifest, social preview image and description metadata were added.
- **Analytics without people.** `client/src/core/analytics.js` sends events only when the host enables them: level started, chapter reached, a stall in an interaction, completion, how a question to Dalil was answered (reviewed / model / referred), a frame-rate bucket. Nothing the player types, no identifiers, no addresses stored.
- **Landscape phones.** Pillar meanings show only for the pillar being met or suggested where the five stand close together on screen; "Start here" sits beside the first light, away from the next name.
- **Tests.** Desktop, phone portrait and phone landscape (Arabic) play-throughs with real mouse and touch events; the full Fitrah level from menu to end card; The Water from the menu through Tafakor to its end and back, also through the production server (no console errors, so nothing blocked by its security policy). `tests/fitrah_validation.mjs`: 698 checks (16 verses, 1 hadith, 143 authored lines). Not yet tested on physical phones.
- **Unchanged on purpose.** Every source is still labelled "Pending Sharia review"; the hadith card still shows no hadith text and marks its numbering "to be confirmed". Chapters 2 and 4 keep their lighter step, marked "first version". "Talk to someone" still says that no person or organisation is connected.

## 5 October

### Fitrah · Tafakor: the menu's two paths, and the first Fitrah level

Implemented on the existing Vite + Three.js build (no framework change), keeping the menu's look and The Water exactly as they were.

- **Menu.** The cinematic menu now opens on two paths, **Fitrah — The Four Questions** and **Tafakor — The Experiential Worlds**. Same still-and-motes atmosphere, type, motion and veil; the backdrop cross-fades to each path's still. Tafakor opens a second list in place (The Water; The Mountains and The Space marked as later) with a way back. Leaving a Tafakor world returns to the Tafakor list (`client/src/shell/`, `client/src/levels/registry.js`).
- **The Fitrah level** (`client/src/levels/fitrah/`): one Chamber of Questions — a circular hall of dark slabs and slits of light, a reflective floor with faint rings, suspended fragments of light — whose state blends with each chapter (creation, purpose, practice, return, then the ending). The opening asks how familiar Islam is to the player (three depths, never stored), four question lights rise, and the player chooses when to begin.
- **Chapter 1, "Who created me?", is fully playable.** The player drags from their own light to what came before them — their parents, those before them, the earth, the sun, the stars — each link named and marked "had a beginning"; the last reaches into the dark and finds nothing that explains itself. Dalil names what they found, 52:35 appears in three layers, then the answer (Allah) and 39:62. The chain is framed inside the safe region on every screen, bends away from Dalil, and links only what the hand reaches for (tested with real mouse and touch drags in portrait and landscape, English and Arabic).
- **Chapters 2–4** have their chamber state, Dalil's introduction, source, answer, reflection and summary (51:56; the hadith of the five pillars with the five pillar names; 3:185 and the reminder of mercy), with a light step in place of their interactions, marked "first version" on screen.
- **Ending:** the four answers gathered under their lights, 30:30 with Dalil's explanation, and four ways on: Tafakor, Ask Dalil, Talk to someone, back to the beginning.
- **Dalil from the first frame:** the same walking light as in The Water, now with a warm aura, light on the floor, footfalls, a glint toward where he looks and an arc of light to what he points at. He walks to the player's side at the start, introduces each question, explains each source at the player's depth ("Simpler" / "More detail" beside every explanation), words instructions for mouse, touch or pen, and can be asked anything at any time (tap him, the Ask button, or `?`). Answers: reviewed content first, then a validated model (server route or the artifact's `sample`), with chapter-specific suggestions and the conversation so far; rulings and distress go to a person, never to the model.
- **Shared core** for every level: `core/sacred/` (verified verses, content types, whole-answer validation), `core/dalil/ask.js` (AI adapters), `core/help/talk.js` ("Talk to someone"). The dev server's Dalil route picks the prompt by level.
- **Sources stay pending.** Every verse, translation and the hadith is labelled "Pending Sharia review". The hadith card shows the collections with their numbering marked "to be confirmed" and no hadith text, which has not been entered from a verified edition; Dalil's summary is labelled as his. The five additions (39:62, 47:19, 3:97, 98:5, the hadith) and the chapter pairings are listed in the content file and in the in-game review panel. Four verses were added to `data/quran/verses.json` from the pinned source (existing text unchanged).
- **Talk to someone is honest:** it says no person or organisation is connected yet, offers only general pathways (a mosque near you, an information centre in your country), lets the player copy their question, and shows emergency guidance first when distress is detected. A verified destination can be added in `data/config/human-help.json`; the panel then asks consent before anything is shared.
- **A catch from the new check:** `tests/fitrah_validation.mjs` found five Arabic lines written for Dalil that reproduced Qur'anic wording (e.g. "الله خالق كل شيء", "من رحمة الله", "من استطاع إليه سبيلا"). They were rewritten, so Qur'anic wording appears only in verified verse records.
- **Tests:** `client/tests/fitrah_playthrough.py` (menu to end card with real drags; desktop, phone portrait, phone landscape in Arabic), `client/tests/fitrah_validation.mjs` (586 checks), The Water through Tafakor and back.

### Design: Fitrah and Tafakor

A new Game Design & Technical Architecture document, "Fitrah — Game Design & Technical Architecture" (a Claude doc), restructures the product into two paths: **Fitrah**, the guided four-question level (Who created me? Allah. Why am I here? To worship Allah. How should I live? Arkan al-Islam. What comes after death? Al-Akhirah), and **Tafakor** (تفكّر), the contemplation worlds, with The Water as the first and The Mountains and The Space as placeholders. It covers the four chapters beat by beat in one transforming "Chamber of Questions", one interaction per chapter, the sources for each answer (mostly already in `data/quran/verses.json`, plus 39:62, 47:19, 3:97, 98:5 and the hadith of the five pillars to add), Dalil as a visible educator with three knowledge levels, source-first answering and validated structured output, an honest "Talk to someone" path, the level registry and content model, performance tiers, acceptance criteria and the implementation priority order.

## 4 October

### The Water: the first scene of Fitrah, a menu of scenes, clearer instructions, mobile framing

The approved build was kept as the reference before anything changed (branch `reference/the-water`). Then, without redesigning the experience:

- **Renamed and organised into levels.** The Revival is now **The Water** (`client/src/levels/water/`), one level of Fitrah. `client/src/levels/registry.js` defines the levels (`id`, `title`, `description`, `status`, scene loader, still); The Light and The Balance are listed as placeholders with no content. A new entry page (`client/fitrah.html`, `client/src/shell/`) holds the menu: a still from The Water, slow motes of light, the name, and the scenes as a quiet list. Choosing The Water lowers a veil with its title while the scene loads behind it; leaving (from the in-game menu or the end card) returns to the menu. No scores, stars or progression.
- **Dalil says what to do with the hands.** Each gesture's instruction is worded for the way the player is touching the screen: "Click and hold, then follow it." with a mouse, "Touch and hold, then drag along it." on a phone, "Press and hold…" with a pen (`client/src/core/device.js`, `dalil/lines.js`). He says it as he points (wind, rain, water, the chain, the thinning cloud), and one reminder after a stall. Silence budgets were adjusted so the chain's explanation is no longer dropped.
- **The four points always on screen.** The chain's points are kept inside a calculated safe interaction region (viewport, safe-area insets, the corner menu, room for a fingertip) by a framing solver (`client/src/core/framing.js`): the authored frame wherever it already fits (desktop, landscape phone), otherwise the least turn, otherwise centred with a wider lens; recalculated when the screen turns or resizes. The same check keeps the thinning cloud reachable in the light sequence. Touch targets and tolerances are a little larger under a finger.
- **Tests:** full play-throughs from the menu with real gestures on desktop and on emulated phones in portrait and landscape (real touch events), checking all four points are inside the screen, then back to the menu and into The Water again (`client/tests/water_full_playthrough.sh`).

### The Revival: the player as witness

A creative correction made the player a witness, never a creator: the player does not make rain, move clouds, bring light, revive the earth or control balance. The prototype (`client/revival.html`, `client/src/revival/`) was rebuilt around it.

- **The world acts on its own time.** The director drives the weather (`sim.js`: `worldGather`, `worldWind`, `worldWet`, `worldThin`). No player input writes into the simulation.
- **Discovery gestures** (`interact.js`): Trace (follow the wind current; follow the first water down the dry stream bed, which is also how the player walks), Reveal (a lens through the rain onto the cracked soil, closing again when attention moves), Connect (cloud, rain, soil, water, flower) and Align (keep the observation field on a cloud as it thins by itself). Gestures change only attention: the camera, the lens, a knot of light. No failure; one gentle observation after a stall; holding Space follows with assistance.
- **Phenomena** (`phenomena.js`): wind filaments and chaff along the current, raindrops striking the soil near the player, Dalil's pointing arc, the glow behind a thinning cloud, the first flower in the meadow.
- **Route:** the valley is now walked downhill (north to south), so following water is physically right.
- **Dalil as a first-class system** (`dalil/brain.js`): guide (notices, points, leads, waits), interpreter (explains what was witnessed and, after reading time, what a verse says) and companion (near, steps aside for revelations, sits beside the player at the end). Lines carry content types and run under a per-sequence silence budget (`dalil/lines.js`).
- **Semantic events and content types** (`events.js`): Dalil, the director, audio and the AI context engine share events such as PLAYER_TRACED_WIND and PLAYER_FINISHED_READING_VERSE. Every text is QURAN_ARABIC, TRANSLATION, DALIL_EXPLANATION, NARRATIVE_DIALOGUE or EDUCATIONAL_CONTEXT, and the interface labels it.
- **Three-layer revelation** (`verse.js`): Arabic, then translation, then Dalil's explanation in a separate labelled card after the reader's time. Verse-first at the stream; Dalil-first-then-verse at the light.
- **AI context engine** (`dalil/prompt.js`): the model receives scene, environmental state, current interaction, current verse, recent events, the last exchanges, approved knowledge and language, and must answer with one of Dalil's content types. Validation now also rejects answers that give the player or Dalil divine agency.
- **Light climax:** the break glows at its rim while the player keeps it in view, then the canopy separates by itself; shafts pour through the whole opening, colours warm, motes light up, exposure breathes and settles.
- **Design document:** section 0 "Creative correction: the player is a witness (binding)" added to the Fitrah 2.0 — The Revival design document; sections 4 and 13 marked as superseded.
- **Tests:** full play-through with real pointer gestures (trace, reveal, connect, align) from arrival to the end card; validation unit check extended (`client/tests/revival_validation.mjs`).
